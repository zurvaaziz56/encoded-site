# Encoded redesign notes

Research pass, November 2026.

Method note: live page fetches were blocked in the research session, so the hero headlines below come from search-indexed snapshots of each site and from third-party listings. Check each one against the live page before quoting it anywhere public.

## StoryBrand 2.0 website guidance

### The grunt test

A stranger should know three things within about 5 seconds of landing:

1. What do you offer?
2. How will it make my life better?
3. How do I buy it?

Miller's image: if a caveman glanced at the page, could he grunt back the answer? The header carries most of this load. A short headline, one line of support and one clear button usually pass. Clever wordplay usually fails.

Sources: [getAbstract summary of Building a StoryBrand](https://www.getabstract.com/en/summary/building-a-storybrand/31696), [StoryBrand wireframe SOP (unofficial)](https://storybrand.pages.dev/sops/website), [Connective: StoryBrand framework](https://connectivewebdesign.com/blog/storybrand-framework)

### The one-liner

Three parts, in order:

- **Problem**: name the customer's problem first. This is the hook.
- **Solution**: say what you do about it, framed as the plan.
- **Result**: describe what life looks like once it is solved.

It should be short enough to say out loud, and it gets reused in the header, the email signature, the bio and the elevator pitch. The method is set out in *Marketing Made Simple* (Miller and J.J. Peterson, 2020).

Sources: [Chatterbuzz: StoryBrand one-liner](https://www.chatterbuzzmedia.com/blog/how-to-write-story-brand-one-liner/), [FenderBender: The marketing one-liner](https://www.fenderbender.com/articles/10943-the-marketing-one-liner), [Marketing Made Simple (book listing)](https://skillsoft.com/book/marketing-made-simple-a-step-by-step-storybrand-guide-for-any-business-e9626e30-dbf3-4930-85ff-8270f64bf143)

### The website wireframe

The *Marketing Made Simple* podcast describes nine sections (third-party guides list anywhere from 7 to 10):

1. **Header**: the offer in a few words, a direct call to action (buy or book) and an optional transitional one (a free resource). This is where the page passes the grunt test.
2. **Stakes**: what it costs the customer not to act. Name the failure they want to avoid.
3. **Value proposition**: the benefits, usually three short, scannable "buckets".
4. **Guide**: empathy plus authority. Show you understand the problem, then prove competence with testimonials, logos, numbers or credentials.
5. **Plan**: three simple steps to buy and get the result. Don't overcomplicate it.
6. **Explanatory paragraph**: two or three sentences, or a slightly longer passage, for readers who want more before they act. It also helps search.
7. **Video** (optional): short, about the problem and how it gets solved.
8. **Price choices**: clear options so the visitor can pick what fits their need and budget.
9. **Junk drawer**: the footer. It holds everything that used to feel important: links, contact, legal, social.

The direct call to action repeats at the header, after the plan and near the end.

Sources: [Marketing Made Simple podcast, ep. 13: A website wireframe that works](https://marketingmadesimple.libsyn.com/13-a-website-wireframe-that-works), [Revision Road: 9 website sections](https://revision-road-site.pages.dev/blog/9-website-sections-youre-probably-getting-wrong), [Girard Media: Seven elements](https://girardmedia.com/blog/storybrand-framework-marketing-messaging), [Knapsack Creative: StoryBrand website framework](https://knapsackcreative.com/blog/a-deep-dive-into-storybrands-website-framework?format=amp), [Five Funnels wireframe guide](https://five-funnels-resources.pages.dev/downloads/website-wireframe-guide.html)

## Competitor and adjacent sites

**Writer** ([writer.com](https://writer.com))
- Hero (indexed banner, late 2025): "The enterprise AI platform for work." Third-party write-ups describe the positioning as "the enterprise AI platform for agentic work".
- Clear on category and buyer (enterprise). Unclear on outcome: it never says what changes for the person writing the draft.
- Avoid: leading with "platform" and "agentic". Borrow: naming the buyer right in the headline.
- Sources: [indexator.ai](https://indexator.ai/startups/writer), [Atlan](https://atlan.com/know/ai-agent/ai-agent-applications/what-is-writer-ai-enterprise-platform.md)

**Markup AI, formerly Acrolinx** ([markup.ai](https://markup.ai), [acrolinx.com](https://www.acrolinx.com))
- Hero (indexed page): "Guardian Agents for all your content." Sub: "Enterprise-grade content guardrails to keep your content on-brand and compliant." The about page says: "Content Guardian Agents make every output right."
- The subhead is clear. The headline is a coined, trademarked category that a busy reader can't decode in 5 seconds.
- Borrow: Acrolinx's older framing of turning "complex style guides into actionable rules" is close to Encoded's idea in plain words. Avoid: inventing a category name.
- Sources: [markup.ai indexed page](https://markup.ai/?p=204), [markup.ai/about](https://markup.ai/about/), [Acrolinx brochure (PDF)](https://www.acrolinx.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/12/Acrolinx-Enterprise-Editorial-Management-Brochure.pdf)

**Jasper** ([jasper.ai](https://www.jasper.ai))
- Hero (indexed): "The AI your marketing deserves." Sub: "Elevate your team, your brand, and your impact with AI that's built for marketing."
- The headline is about emotion, not substance. The sub uses filler verbs. The brand voice promise sits further down: "Configure Jasper so that each output adheres to your brand's unique voice, tone, and style."
- Borrow: that plain voice, tone and style promise. Avoid: "elevate"-style verbs, and burying the most specific claim below the fold.
- Sources: [jasper.ai](https://jasper.ai/), [Thinking Box case study](https://thinkingbox.com/work/jasper-ai-website)

**Main & Machine** ([mainandmachine.com](https://www.mainandmachine.com)), AI implementation studio
- Hero (indexed): "The machine belongs to Main Street." Tagline: "AI implementation for small & mid-size business." Sprint page: "We build the working systems."
- The headline is clever but needs the tagline to explain it. Strong on buying clarity: it publishes a fixed price range ($18,000 to $60,000, quoted before work begins), says every engagement starts with an assessment, and promises to keep building if a scoped workflow isn't live within 90 days.
- Borrow: the published price and the risk reversal. Avoid: a headline that fails the grunt test without its sub.
- Sources: [homepage](https://www.mainandmachine.com/), [Implementation sprint](https://www.mainandmachine.com/services/implementation-sprint/)

**Just Think AI** ([justthink.ai](https://justthink.ai)), AI implementation studio
- Hero headline not captured. The sprint offer is a two-week, fixed-fee build that takes "one bounded workflow" and ships it ($25k to $60k).
- Clear because the scope is small and named. That framing maps directly onto a 7-day, fixed-price audit.
- Borrow: tight scope language ("one workflow, fixed fee, fixed time"). Avoid: engineering-led copy that a PR reader would skim past.
- Source: [Sprint pricing](https://justthink.ai/pricing/sprint)

**Muck Rack** ([muckrack.com](https://muckrack.com)), PR software the target customer already uses
- Positioning (third-party profiles): "AI-powered PR software built for how brands and agencies work". The older tagline: "Smarter PR with powerful, easy-to-use software."
- "Built for how agencies work" respects the reader's existing workflow. "Smarter PR" is generic.
- Borrow: speak to how PR teams already work rather than asking them to adopt a new system. Avoid: "smarter" claims with no proof.
- Sources: [Built In profile](https://www.builtincolorado.com/company/muck-rack), [Product Hunt](https://www.producthunt.com/products/muck-rack/embed)

**Agency Management Institute** ([agencymanagementinstitute.com](https://agencymanagementinstitute.com)), service firm for small agency owners
- Hero not captured. It describes itself as helping owners of small and mid-sized marketing communication companies "move up to their next performance level". Its story line: most agency owners are "accidental business owners".
- The "accidental business owners" line is a textbook StoryBrand guide move: empathy first, naming the reader's situation before selling.
- Borrow: one empathy line that names exactly how a small agency feels about AI. Avoid: vague "next level" outcomes.
- Sources: [Meet AMI](https://agencymanagementinstitute.com/meet-ami/), [Agency consulting](https://agencymanagementinstitute.com/advertising-agency-consulting/)

**NisonCo** ([nisonco.com](https://nisonco.com)), PR firm offering AI consulting
- Hero not captured. Its AI consulting helps firms "identify the right tools and develop strategic implementation plans".
- It is clear, but it sells advice and plans, so the client still does the work.
- Borrow: nothing visual. Use it as a contrast: Encoded's difference is done-for-you setup, with rules actually encoded rather than a plan handed over.
- Source: [How to use AI to generate media coverage](https://nisonco.com/how-to-use-ai-to-generate-media-coverage)

## Motion and visual craft references

**Apple AirPods Pro product page** ([apple.com/airpods-pro](https://www.apple.com/airpods-pro/))
- Technique: a scroll-scrubbed canvas. Scroll progress maps to a frame index, which is redrawn on a pinned canvas. Frames are preloaded to avoid network stutter.
- For Encoded: use the same scrub mechanic, but have the scene drawn in code (a draft whose markup resolves as you scroll), not a heavy image sequence.
- Sources: [CSS-Tricks tutorial](https://css-tricks.com/?p=308477), [Geyer: CSS image sequence animations](https://hn.jakechampion.name/reader/https://geyer.dev/blog/css-image-sequence-animations)

**The Pudding** ([pudding.cool](https://pudding.cool))
- Technique: the sticky graphic pattern. A graphic pins with CSS `position: sticky` while text "steps" scroll past, each step triggering a state change through IntersectionObserver (their Scrollama library).
- For Encoded: pin a "house style" panel while the three plan steps scroll past it.
- Sources: [Scrollytelling sticky](https://pudding.cool/process/scrollytelling-sticky/), [Introducing Scrollama](https://pudding.cool/process/introducing-scrollama/)

**Stripe** ([stripe.com](https://stripe.com))
- Technique: one ambient WebGL canvas layer (the animated mesh gradient, built on a tiny "minigl" wrapper, with colours set by CSS variables). Known to cost battery.
- For Encoded: at most one ambient canvas, paused when offscreen and off under `prefers-reduced-motion`. Skip the gradient itself, since it now reads as the generic AI-startup look.
- Sources: [Bram.us: Stripe gradient effect](https://www.bram.us/2021/10/13/how-to-create-the-stripe-website-gradient-effect), [GSAP forum thread](https://gsap.com/community/forums/topic/24837-animating-gradients/)

**Linear** ([linear.app](https://linear.app))
- Technique: a near-monochrome palette, restrained motion and a long-scroll "Method" section that explains a philosophy in type, without product shots. Reimplementations copy its scroll-linked scale-down with gradient fades on stacked panels.
- For Encoded: let typography carry the idea. A teardown suggests clamping animation duration (around 250ms) when the user scrolls fast, so the motion can't be outrun.
- Sources: [Linear Method teardown](https://webtrends.pages.dev/posts/linears-method-page-teardown), [Product Hunt discussion](https://www.producthunt.com/p/linear/what-do-you-think-of-linear-s-new-homepage)

**Vercel** ([vercel.com](https://vercel.com))
- Technique: restrained colour (black, white, a ladder of greys), tight negative letter-spacing on a large display headline, fine-line grid structure and one typeface family (Geist).
- For Encoded: fine rules and a visible grid, like a printed style guide, with one accent colour used sparingly.
- Sources: [Vercel homepage snapshot](https://vercel.com/home), [Tenet: Vercel homepage](https://www.wearetenet.com/inspirations/saas-design-inspiration/saas-homepage-design-examples/vercel-homepage)

## Takeaways for Encoded

- **The gap is plain language plus a person.** The tools sell "platforms", "agents" and "guardrails" to enterprises. No one is telling a 2-15 person PR team, in their own words, that someone will set up their AI to follow their house style for them.
- **The header must pass the grunt test on its own.** What: AI set up to follow your editorial rules. Better life: drafts come out right first time, with no rewrite passes. How to buy: one button for the $1,500, 7-day audit. No coined category names and no wordplay that needs a subhead to decode.
- **Draft one-liner to test:** "Boutique PR teams using AI still lose hours rewriting drafts that miss the house style. Encoded sets up your AI to follow your own editorial rules, so drafts come out right first time."
- **Put the price on the page.** Enterprise tools hide pricing behind demos. The implementation studios that convert well publish a fixed price and a fixed scope. "$1,500, fixed, 7 days" is a selling point, and it belongs in the header call to action and the price section.
- **Use PR words, not AI words.** Voice, house style, formats, client-ready, rewrite pass. Cut "agentic", "guardrails", "elevate", "unlock", "platform".
- **Guide section: empathy first, then authority.** One line that names how small agencies feel about AI drafts, then Zurva Aziz as copywriter and content strategist (not "founder"), with proof such as testimonials or before-and-after drafts, cleared for public use.
- **One signature motion idea, built in code.** For example, a pinned scene where a generic AI draft is marked up in editorial red and resolves into on-voice copy as you scroll (scroll-scrubbed canvas or SVG, sticky pattern). Surround it with stillness: a fine-line grid, an ink and paper palette with one accent, and a serif display face paired with a clean sans. Avoid gradient blobs, glowing orbs and purple AI styling.
- **Craft rules for the build:** `position: sticky` and IntersectionObserver over scroll listeners. Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`. Pause canvases offscreen. Clamp animation durations so fast scrolling never breaks the scene. The page must still pass the grunt test with motion turned off.

## Design decisions

**Message.** The header says what it is in one line ("AI drafts that follow your house style. Every time."), the sub-line says how (a system that checks each draft against your own rules before anyone reads it) and the button says what to do next (book a 30-minute call). The controlling idea ("your rules should hold on every draft") drives the rest of the page rather than the headline, because the headline has to pass the grunt test on its own.

**StoryBrand map.**

| Section | StoryBrand role |
| --- | --- |
| Hero | Header: offer, direct CTA (book) and transitional CTA (form), price and audience in one line |
| The problem | Villain (the rewrite pass), internal problem first, philosophical line last |
| What we build | Value proposition, shown as drafts passing through your rules |
| How it starts | Plan: Day 1, Days 2 to 5, Day 7 |
| Running today | Guide authority: the three live systems |
| From babysitting to trusting | Stakes and success side by side (stakes kept to three short lines) |
| Pricing | Price choices, with the agreement plan in the fine print and the FAQ |
| Who does the work | Guide empathy (the quote) plus authority (two years, 100+ Upwork projects, Top Rated Plus) |
| Questions, final CTA, footer | Objections, repeated direct CTA, junk drawer |

**Look.** Paper and ink with one editor's red, used only for problems being flagged. A pass green appears only when something is fixed. No gradients, blobs or glowing shapes. The dark sections are flat near-black, like a printed reverse.

**Type.** Newsreader (a variable newspaper serif with real optical sizes and a strong italic) for display, Geist for text and Geist Mono for labels and the rule lists, so the "system" voice reads differently from the "editorial" voice. All three are self-hosted in `/fonts` (latin subset, variable woff2, SIL Open Font License), so there is no third-party font request and the hero can preload them.

**Motion, all built in code.**
- Hero: a draft is scanned, four rule breaks are flagged in red (headline count, AI filler opening, US spelling for a UK title, an em dash), each is fixed, and the rules panel ends on "6 of 6 rules hold". It loops every 14 seconds and pauses when off screen.
- Hero background: a canvas of fine ruled lines, loose on the left and straight on the right, drawn at device resolution and nudged by the pointer. One red line straightens with them.
- The problem: the statement fills in word by word as you scroll, then a red pencil circles "everything".
- What we build: a sticky, scroll-scrubbed scene. Monday to Friday drafts, tilted and marked in red, pass through a "Your rules" gate and come out straight with a tick. The rules light up one by one as each draft passes.
- Plan: the red timeline draws as you read. Proof cards carry small live diagrams (8 stages lighting in turn, 200 dots, a 14-day publishing grid).
- No animation library. Everything is vanilla JS, CSS and canvas, driven by one requestAnimationFrame scroll handler and IntersectionObserver.

**Reduced motion.** With `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`, every scene renders its finished state: the hero draft is already corrected, the sticky scene becomes a static before-and-after, the circle is drawn and the timeline is complete.

**Performance.** No external scripts. The canvases pause off screen and when the tab is hidden. The hero illustration has a fixed layout (old and new text are stacked in the same grid cell), so corrections never push content around. The portrait is served as WebP with a JPEG fallback and lazy-loaded.

**`/for-pr-agencies`.** Kept as a short page in the new system, for outreach links that already point to it: the ten kinds of checks as a rulebook, and the audit offer.
