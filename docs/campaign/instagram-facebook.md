# Instagram and Facebook plan: @getencoded and the Encoded page

Six weeks of posts, Stories, Facebook reuse, a Groups plan and an optional small paid boost for the House Rules Kit.

Captions are written in Zurva's voice (first person, "I"). She is a copywriter and content strategist in London. Every fact in this file comes from the campaign brief. If a post needs a number, a client or a result the brief does not give, it does not get one.

## The look: aurora glass

Every visual in this plan uses the same system. Each post's visual spec below says which parts it leans on.

- **Background:** soft aurora light in warm peach (#FFB49A), apricot (#FFE0A8), mint (#A9E8CC) and sky (#BFDDFF), blurred, drifting from one corner. Base cream #FFF9F5. Each post is either "warm" (peach and apricot lead) or "cool" (mint and sky lead).
- **Cards:** frosted glass cards, rounded corners, a thin white edge, a faint shadow. Text always sits on a card, never straight on the aurora.
- **Type:** Plus Jakarta Sans, bold, near-black (#1D1A21) for headlines. Body in the same family, regular weight, #4C4553.
- **Labels:** small monospace labels in lowercase, top-left of the card, like `red pen friday · 01` or `ai wrote`. These carry the series name and slide numbers.
- **Marks:** a green tick (#0E7C66) means "passes". A red underline (#C8342B), hand-drawn feel, means "breaks". Use them on the exact word that passes or breaks, never as decoration.
- **Format:** design at 1080 x 1350 (4:5). Keep the headline inside the central 3:4 area (about 1012 x 1350) so it survives the profile grid crop. Reels at 1080 x 1920 with on-screen text inside the middle 4:5 area so the cover crop works.
- **Sentence case** everywhere, on images too. UK spelling. No em dashes on any slide.

**How they look together:**

- **A checkerboard of light.** Warm tiles (peach and apricot) and cool tiles (mint and sky) alternate, so the grid reads as one soft sheet of aurora light with a gentle pattern, even when you scroll past it fast.
- **One headline per tile.** Every cover has a single near-black headline of six words or fewer on one frosted card. No tile has two competing messages.
- **Marks run the middle column.** The middle column (posts 8, 5 and 2) each carry a big red underline on the cover. The corners (1, 3, 7 and 9) each carry a green tick. From a distance the grid shows red down the centre, green at the corners: the whole product in one glance.
- **Labels in the same spot.** Every cover has its monospace series label in the top-left corner of the card, so the grid feels like one publication with sections.
- **Reels blend in.** All three Reel covers (2, 6 and 8) are designed like carousel covers, so the grid never shows a random video frame.

**Pins (from week 4):** pin post 9 (the explainer), post 13 (the sample kit Reel) and, from week 5, post 19 (which kit?). Pinned posts become the top row, so keep the warm, cool, warm cover order when you choose them.

### Facebook Groups strategy

Groups are where PR people ask each other for help. That makes them the best place to be useful and the worst place to sell. Post as Zurva from her personal profile, with "copywriter and content strategist, building editorial AI systems" in her profile intro and getencoded.co in her profile links. People who want to know more will click through.

#### Six types of groups to join

1. **PR and comms practitioner groups.** Large, general groups for people working in PR and communications. Lots of questions about press releases, pitching and media coverage, and more and more about AI tools.
2. **Small agency owner groups.** Groups for people running agencies of a handful of staff. Talk is about hiring, pricing, client management and keeping quality steady while the team grows.
3. **Freelance writer and copywriter groups.** Freelancers comparing rates, clients and workflows. Many now use AI for first drafts and swap tips on fixing them.
4. **AI tools for marketers and comms groups.** Groups focused on using ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini or Copilot at work. People share prompts and complain about the output, which is exactly the conversation the kit answers.
5. **In-house and internal comms groups.** Comms leads at companies and charities. Smaller teams, lots of stakeholders, house style questions about tone and terminology.
6. **Editing, proofreading and style groups.** Editors and grammar enthusiasts debating spelling, punctuation and style guides. Great for UK versus US spelling and dash debates.

Pick two or three to start, read them for a week before posting, and read every set of group rules properly. Many ban links in posts entirely.

#### How to add value without spamming

- **Answer before you post.** Spend the first two weeks only replying to other people's questions about AI drafts, house style and editing. Give the full answer in the comment. No teasers.
- **No links in posts or comments** unless the group rules allow them and someone has asked.
- **Share the thinking. Leave the product out.** Red pen Friday style examples work brilliantly in groups when they are posted as plain text with no branding.
- **Ask real questions.** "How does your team handle client-specific house styles?" starts a useful thread and teaches you how your buyers talk.
- **Never copy and paste the same post into several groups.** Admins notice, and so do members who are in more than one.
- **Credit the group.** If a thread gives you a great example, ask before you use it on Instagram, and remove names.

#### Four example value posts (no selling)

**Value post 1. The count check**

> A tiny habit that has saved me a lot of red pen: when an AI tool writes a list post, I check the headline number last, not first. AI drafts often add or drop an item while writing and never go back to the headline. "5 tips" with 6 tips is the most common one I see. Does anyone else have a last-step check they always do?

**Value post 2. Titles**

> Small thing, but it matters to clients: AI tools love to call spokespeople "experts". "Finance expert", "marketing expert", "industry leader", "thought leader". If you give the AI the person's exact title in the prompt ("chartered financial planner", "head of research"), it usually sticks to it. If you don't, it makes one up. How do you all keep spokesperson titles straight across clients?

**Value post 3. Spelling per client**

> For anyone writing for UK and US outlets at once: I keep a one-line spelling rule per client at the top of every AI prompt. "UK spelling. Organisation, programme, analyse, centre." Naming a few example words works better for me than just saying "UK English". Curious whether others do this per client or per outlet.

**Value post 4. Quotes**

> One rule I'd put in every PR house style: never let AI edit a direct quote. It will "improve" "We're pleased" into "We're absolutely thrilled" without blinking, and the client will notice. If a quote needs a change, flag it and ask. What's the worst quote change you've caught?

#### When it is OK to mention the kit

- **When someone asks directly** for a tool, template or service for AI house style. Answer the question properly first, then say: "Full disclosure, I build these. There's a free sample kit at getencoded.co if it helps."
- **On promotion days or threads** the group runs for members' services. Follow the format the admins ask for.
- **When an admin has said yes.** If you want to share the free Fernhill PR sample kit, message the admin first and ask.
- **In DMs, only when invited.** If someone messages you to ask what you do, tell them. Never cold-DM group members.
- **Never** in reply to someone's complaint about a bad AI draft, unless they ask what you would use. Sympathy first, always.

Always say it is yours. "I build these" is one sentence and keeps your reputation in the group.
