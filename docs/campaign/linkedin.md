## 3. Engagement playbook

### Who I comment on

- **PR agency founders and managing directors** at 2 to 15 person agencies, London and the rest of the UK first. I comment on their posts about process, hiring writers, client service and AI, and skip the award photos.
- **Comms directors and heads of communications** in small in-house teams. They post about approvals and spokesperson prep, which is all house-rule territory.
- **PR trade editors and writers** at titles like PRWeek, PRmoment and Influence. Their posts on AI in PR get read by exactly the people I want to reach, and a useful comment there does more than a post of my own.
- **Journalists who post about bad pitches.** They share the worst press release lines. I add the rule that would have caught it, with no pitch.
- **Secondary:** freelance PR writers and content agency leads, who feel the same pain on a smaller scale.

I keep about 30 of these people on a list and ring the bell on their profiles, so their posts show up in my notifications.

### How I comment

Two to four sentences. Something specific from real PR work. A question at the end when it's natural. No links, no mention of the kit, no "great post".

### Five example comments

1. **On an agency MD's post about using AI in the team:**
   "The draft is the quick bit. The time goes on the read-through afterwards: spelling against the outlet and titles against the approved list. Have you ever written down what that read-through checks for? That list is usually most of a house style already."
2. **On a comms director's post about approvals:**
   "Approved wording is the one place AI shouldn't improvise. Worth putting spokesperson titles and signed-off lines into your AI tools as fixed text, so the draft can't 'improve' them on the way through."
3. **On a journalist's post about bad pitches:**
   "'In today's fast-paced world' should trigger an automatic out-of-office. Out of interest, what's the one phrase that makes you stop reading?"
4. **On a PR trade editor's article about AI in agencies:**
   "Useful piece. The bit I'd add: most of the risk sits in tiny things, like a US spelling in a UK outlet or a job title the client never approved. Easy to fix, easy to miss, which is why someone senior ends up rereading every draft."
5. **On a freelance PR writer's post about client quirks:**
   "Every client has one rule nobody wrote down until it was broken. Do you keep a running list per client? Even a note on your phone saves the second argument."

### Daily 20-minute routine

- **Minutes 0 to 5:** reply to every comment on my latest post. A proper sentence each time.
- **Minutes 5 to 15:** leave 4 or 5 thoughtful comments from my list of 30, starting with whoever posted in the last hour.
- **Minutes 15 to 18:** send up to 5 connection requests from my outreach list (section 4), each with a personal line.
- **Minutes 18 to 20:** send the sample to anyone who commented RULES, reply to DMs, and update my tracking sheet.

On posting days I do the routine within an hour of the post going live, so I'm around for the first comments.

- Company size: "2-10 employees" and "11-50 employees". The second bucket is wide, so I open each page and keep the ones showing 15 or fewer people.
- Location: London first, then United Kingdom, then Ireland.
- From each company page, "People" tab: the managing director, the head of content or editorial, and the account directors.

- `("account director" OR "associate director") AND ("boutique PR" OR "PR agency")`

- Company headcount: 1-10 and 11-50

### Rules

- **No spam.** No more than 10 connection requests a day, no automation tools, no bulk sends.
- **Personalise one line** in every message, taken from something real on their profile or in their posts. If I can't find one, I don't send.
- **No links in the connection note.** The sample goes in message 2, and only if they say yes.
- **Stop after 3.** No reply after message 3 means no. A "no thanks" at any point gets a "thanks for saying" and nothing more.
- **Keep a simple sheet:** name, agency, team size, date of each message, outcome. It stops double messages and tells me which openers work.
