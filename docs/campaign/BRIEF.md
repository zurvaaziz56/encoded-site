# Campaign brief (shared by every writer)

## The product
Encoded (getencoded.co) sells the **House Rules Kit**: a PR team answers a 15-minute questionnaire and sends 3 to 5 published pieces; in 2 business days they get their house style built into their AI tools (Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini, Copilot) plus a checker that catches every rule break in any draft. Prices: **$99** kit, **$249** Calibrated Kit (tuned to their real edits after 2 weeks, plus a 30-minute call), custom systems for bigger agencies (book a call). Free sample kit for a made-up agency ("Fernhill PR") can be downloaded on the site. Order at getencoded.co.

## Who it's for
People responsible for editorial quality at PR agencies and in-house comms teams of 2 to 15 people, who already use AI and spend their days fixing its drafts. Secondary: freelance PR writers, content agencies, comms leads.

## Their pain (in their words)
AI drafts look fine until they don't. The headline says "5 tips" and there are 6. US spelling for a UK outlet. "Finance expert" instead of the client's real title. "In today's fast-paced world." Made-up labels like "Tip:". Em dashes everywhere. Someone rereads every draft, usually the most senior person. Every client has a slightly different house style.

## Who's talking
Zurva Aziz, a copywriter and content strategist in London. Never call her a "founder". She has spent two years building editorial AI systems that run in production, has 100+ Upwork projects and Top Rated Plus. Live systems she runs (client names private): an 8-stage editorial QA pipeline for a PR agency (around 20 checks per piece, weekly batches); podcast content packages for a podcast magazine (200+ episodes shipped); a LinkedIn engagement engine for a growth agency (publishing daily). Use ONLY these facts. No invented clients, testimonials, numbers, case results, logos or awards.

## Voice
Simple, clear, witty, warm, a little dry. Talks like a sharp editor at the pub, not a SaaS ad. Short sentences. Concrete examples from real PR work (counts, credentials, spelling, labels, quotes). Jokes come from the specific absurdity of AI drafts, never from hype.

## Hard bans
- No em dashes (use full stops, commas or colons).
- No AI-slop or hype words: game-changer, revolutionise, unlock, elevate, supercharge, leverage, seamless, delve, navigate, landscape, journey, empower, cutting-edge, "in today's fast-paced world" (except when mocking it, in quotes), "not X, but Y" framing, rule-of-three lists for rhythm, rhetorical "Here's the thing", "Let that sink in", emoji walls, hashtag walls (max 3 hashtags, only where the platform rewards them).
- No fake urgency, no fake scarcity, no "only 3 spots".
- Sentence case. UK English (Zurva is in London), prices in $ as on the site.

## Recurring series you may use
- **Red Pen Friday:** one real-looking AI draft line, the fix, and the rule behind it.
- **"AI wrote / Editor wrote":** two-line before and after.
- **Rule of the week:** one house rule, why it matters, how the kit enforces it.
- **Count the items:** a headline that promises 5, a list with 6. Can you spot it?
- **The rewrite pass:** the villain. Never the AI itself, never the writers.
