What you're actually buying

Not a prompt, not a template, not another tool to log into. A system that sits between your source material and your editor, doing the same three things every time.

  1. 📥 Goes in

    Your rules and your raw material

    House style, client voice, formats, banned words. Plus the interviews, transcripts and briefs a piece gets built from.

  2. ⚙️ The system

    It drafts, then checks its own work

    It writes to your formats, then checks the draft against your rules, the same way every time. What breaks a rule gets fixed before a person opens it.

  3. 📤 Comes out

    A draft that already passes

    Plus a record of what was checked. Your editor spends the time on the story instead of the style guide.

None of this replaces your writers. It removes the pass where they fix the same things every week, and it holds on run one hundred.

Fair questions

Will this replace our writers?

No, and we would push back on anyone selling you that. What it removes is the pass where your team fixes the same formatting and voice problems every week. Judgment, story sense and the client relationship stay with people, and a person still signs off on everything that goes out.

What do you need from us to start?

One workflow that hurts, 10 to 20 pieces you have already published, and 30 minutes. If your rules only live in one editor's head, the intake call is where we write them down. That hour is usually the most useful part of the audit.

Which AI tools do you work with?

Whatever your team already uses. Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini, or a mix. The system lives in your rules and your workflow, so it works wherever your team does. You don't need to switch tools.

How do you handle our clients' confidential material?

The audit runs on work you have already published, so nothing sensitive has to move at that stage. Before a build we agree the rest in writing: your NDA, which tools and AI providers process your material, where it lives, and what gets deleted at the end. What we can tell you today →

Why only three spots?

Because I do the work myself and won't rush it. Three concurrent clients is the honest limit for doing this properly.

Do we own what you build?

Yes. All of it. The rules, the documentation, the workflow files. If we ever part ways, everything keeps working and it's all yours.

How is this different from hiring a prompt engineer?

A prompt is a request. We build the system around it: your rules written down properly, tested against your real content, and kept up to date as AI tools change. The difference shows up on run one hundred, not run one.
